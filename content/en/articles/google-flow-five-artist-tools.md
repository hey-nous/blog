## What happened

Google announced five new tools for ==Google Flow==, its AI creative studio, on November 12, 2026. The unusual part is who built them: not a product team shipping features from a roadmap, but five working artists and filmmakers who each made a tool for their own work and then shared it inside Flow.

The headline pair tackles ==character consistency==, the weakness that still gives AI video away. Character Poster, built by artist and filmmaker James Gerde, turns a simple story idea into a polished, styled visual, and helps creators keep one signature look across their social stories. Gerde treats his feeds like curated galleries, where a consistent appearance is the whole point, and Google says the tool can save hours of production work.

Character Arc, built by filmmaker Anurag Tiwari, works on a longer timeline. It shows the same character at different ages and life stages without losing their core look, so a storyteller can develop a character before full production begins. Tiwari is using it on his own documentary, Children of the Sun, which pairs present-day interviews with events from 1974, to trace its protagonist from childhood through adulthood while keeping him recognizable as the same person.

## The other three tools

The rest of the set covers style, light, and footage. Pixel Machine, also built by Tiwari, converts images into bitmap, ASCII, CRT, pixel art, and glitch styles, aimed at title treatments, transitions, and music video visuals. Lucid Glass, designed by artist and creative director Ezra Paulekas, recreates the way light passes through glass, generating prisms, halos, refractions, and optical blooms. Google describes it as an impossible lens, producing effects no real glass could capture.

Pocket Studio, built by visual development artist and filmmaker Ryan McCoy, is the most practical of the five for everyday creators. It takes a single phone take and generates multiple camera angles from it, while upgrading the visual quality and keeping the actor's original performance intact. McCoy has spent more than two decades in previsualization and visual effects for major motion pictures, and the tool reads like a piece of that craft handed to a phone camera.

:::info What is Google Flow?
Flow is Google's AI creative studio for video, built on its Veo video model and Gemini. It is where filmmakers and creators assemble clips, characters, and scenes with AI assistance, rather than generating one-off videos. The new tools live inside Flow as ready-made workflows you can open and run.
:::

## What it means for you

If you make videos with AI, the two character tools are the ones that matter. A recurring character that changes face between shots is still the fastest way to spot AI-generated footage, and it is the reason mascots, presenters, and brand characters have been hard to carry across a series. Character Poster aims at the short form, keeping one look steady across social posts. Character Arc aims at the long form, keeping one person recognizable across decades. Neither is a guarantee, and Google's announcement shows curated examples rather than raw outputs, so the honest test is to run your own character through both and compare frames side by side.

Pocket Studio is the tool most readers can try today with footage they already have. One phone take becoming several angles is a real workflow change for solo creators, who usually shoot a scene once and live with the single angle they got. The claim to watch is the one Google makes about the performance: that the actor's original delivery survives the angle generation. That is a promise about the output, not a measured result, and it is worth checking on a take with strong facial expression or fast movement before trusting it on a finished piece.

:::ringkasan
Google added five artist-built tools to Flow on November 12: Character Poster and Character Arc for keeping one character consistent, Pixel Machine for retro styles, Lucid Glass for glass-light effects, and Pocket Studio for turning one phone take into multiple angles. They are available to try in Flow now, and pre-built tools do not need a paid plan.
:::

Access is the part Google left vague in its announcement. The company says you can try the tools in Flow today, and reporting based on Google's Flow help pages says pre-built tools are usable without a subscription, while building your own custom tool needs a Google AI Plus, Pro, or Ultra plan. Free users get 50 Flow credits a day, and generated media spends those credits. Google has not said how many credits each new tool costs per run, so a heavy session could still hit the daily limit.

:::peringatan
These are announced capabilities shown in Google's own examples, not independently tested results. Character consistency, multi-angle generation, and performance preservation all need a real run with your own footage before you plan a project around them.
:::

What is still unclear: which Google AI plans, if any, get extra access to the new tools, how the credit costs compare with Flow's existing generation, and whether the tools work the same in every region where Flow is available. The answers will come from creators trying them on real projects, and from whether the character tools hold up outside Google's curated demos.
