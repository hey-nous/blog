## What happened

Apple announced on October 31, 2026 that ==Apple Intelligence== is finally coming to mainland China, more than two years after it debuted elsewhere. Testing opens November 4 with the latest iOS developer beta, and the features ship to everyone with iOS 27.3 in December. The announcement came from Apple's China Newsroom, in Chinese.

The headline feature list will look familiar to iPhone owners outside China. The first wave includes Live Translation in Messages, FaceTime, and Phone, Writing Tools, Visual Intelligence, Clean Up in Photos, Genmoji, natural-language photo search, Memories movies, Priority Messages and summaries in Mail, Smart Reply, notification summaries and priority notifications, the Reduce Interruptions Focus, and summaries of transcribed calls and Notes recordings. Siri also gains Type to Siri, which Apple says keeps context across a conversation even when the user stumbles over words.

The China-specific twist is the partner. Apple says it has teamed up with Alibaba to add support for ==Qwen==, Alibaba's large language model, in some Apple Intelligence experiences. Siri can hand certain requests to Qwen to draw on its broad knowledge, users can ask questions about photos and documents such as PDFs and presentations, and a Compose feature in Writing Tools lets Qwen write original content from scratch. Users sign in with a Qwen account or with Sign in with Apple.

## Why a local model was required

Apple Intelligence runs on Apple's own generative models, and Apple is explicit that Qwen is added to selected experiences rather than replacing them. But bringing any AI service to China meant clearing local rules first. China's cyberspace regulator registered Apple's on-device generative AI service for iPhones in July 2026, clearing a major regulatory hurdle, and the partnership with a local model provider is the other half of that equation: outside China, Apple Intelligence routes some requests to ChatGPT, while inside China the equivalent surface routes to Qwen.

That makes this a landmark for the Chinese AI industry as much as for Apple. Qwen becomes the chatbot-style brain inside Siri and the system-wide Writing Tools on every supported iPhone in China, a distribution deal no Chinese model has had before. For Apple, it closes a competitive gap: iPhone owners in one of its biggest markets have gone without its marquee software feature while local brands shipped their own AI assistants.

## What it means for you

If you use an iPhone in China, the practical news is a date and a device list. The December release covers iPhone 16 and later models, iPhone Duo, iPhone 15 Pro and Pro Max, and iPhone Air, with Siri and device language set to English or Simplified Chinese. Eligibility covers users who bought an Apple Intelligence-capable iPhone in China, or whose device location and Apple Account country or region are both set to China. If you are outside China, nothing changes: this rollout is specific to mainland China, and the Qwen integration does not appear in other regions.

On privacy, the two companies have drawn a clear line, at least on paper. Apple asks for consent before any question, document, or image is sent to Qwen, according to a statement published on Alibaba's WeChat account, and Alibaba says it will not use user requests or generated responses for model training. That opt-in design matters because the Qwen requests leave the device, unlike the on-device features that never do.

:::peringatan
Do not read this as Apple Intelligence fully arriving in China, or as Qwen taking over Siri. The December release is a first wave of earlier-generation features: it excludes Siri AI and the other next-generation features Apple announced at WWDC 2026, and Apple has said nothing about when those arrive in China. Mac and iPad support also waits on further approvals, so for now this is an iPhone-only launch.
:::

What is still unclear: the exact December date for iOS 27.3, when Mac and iPad users in China get Apple Intelligence, when Siri AI and the WWDC 2026 features follow, which specific Qwen model version is used (Apple names no version), and the financial terms of the Apple-Alibaba partnership, which neither company disclosed.
