## What happened

Meta announced on October 26, 2026 that it will raise up to $95 billion in new equity to fund its AI expansion, in what Reuters calls the company's largest-ever equity fundraising. The plan comes in two parts: about $35 billion in underwritten offerings launched immediately, and a further program of up to $60 billion planned for 2027.

The immediate offerings are split between $22.5 billion of Class A common stock, sold in connection with ==forward sale agreements==, and about $12.5 billion of depositary shares. Each depositary share represents a 1/20th interest in a share of newly issued Series A Mandatory Convertible Preferred Stock. Meta says the money supports continued investment in AI infrastructure and personal superintelligence, the term it uses for its most ambitious AI goal.

The second, larger part is not yet a live offering. Meta plans an at-the-market program of up to $60 billion in its new non-voting Class C shares, expected to begin in the first quarter of 2027, once those shares begin trading. Meta says it has not finalized the timing or terms of that program, so the $95 billion figure is a ceiling across both parts, not cash already raised.

## Why Meta needs the money now

The context is a spending curve that has outrun the business that used to fund it. Meta expects capital expenditures of $135 billion to $145 billion in 2026, driven by data centers and computing capacity for AI. Meta says it will fund its investments through a mix of operating cash flow, investment-grade debt, strategic partnerships, and equity. This raise is the equity leg of that mix, and it marks a visible shift: a company that spent years buying back its own stock is now selling new shares to keep building.

The buyer list adds a second story. Nvidia, AMD, and Broadcom have indicated non-binding interest in buying up to $5 billion combined of the Class A shares. Those indications are not commitments, and any of the three could buy more, fewer, or none. But the interest itself underlines a circular pattern in the AI economy: the chip suppliers selling Meta the hardware for its data centers may also help finance the purchase.

## What it means for you

If you use Meta's products, nothing changes on the surface: this is a financing decision, not a product announcement. The signal is about the economics underneath AI. The largest AI builders are no longer funding their buildouts from profits alone; they are tapping equity markets at record scale, and Meta is now the biggest example. Alphabet raised $84.75 billion in equity offerings earlier this year, and Meta's plan goes further.

For investors, the key detail is dilution with a twist. The new Class C shares carry no votes, a structure designed to let Meta raise large amounts of equity without diluting founder Mark Zuckerberg's voting control to the same extent. Existing shareholders still face more shares outstanding, which is why the stock fell in morning trading after the announcement, but the voting balance at the top does not move in the same way.

:::peringatan
Do not read the $95 billion as money already in the bank. Only the $35 billion in offerings has launched, and the Class A portion arrives through forward sale agreements, meaning Meta receives those proceeds when the agreements settle later, not upfront. The $60 billion program is planned, unfinalized, and months away from starting.
:::

What is still unclear: the final terms of the $60 billion program, including its exact size and start date, and whether Nvidia, AMD, and Broadcom follow through on their indicated interest. The bigger open question is the one analysts flagged after the announcement: whether this raise is enough, or whether AI's capital appetite keeps growing faster than even $95 billion can cover.
