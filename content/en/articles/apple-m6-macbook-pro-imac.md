## What happened

Apple announced on October 27, 2026 that the M6 chip is coming to two more Macs: the 14-inch MacBook Pro and the 24-inch iMac. Both go up for pre-order on October 28 in 35 countries and regions, with availability starting November 6, and both keep the US starting prices of the models they replace.

The MacBook Pro gets the full M6: a 12-core CPU and a 12-core GPU, plus a ==Dual 16-core Neural Engine==, the dedicated AI hardware Apple uses for on-device machine learning. Apple says graphics are up to 30 percent faster and AI workloads up to 25 percent faster than the M5 model, with battery life of up to 24 hours, which it calls the longest ever in a Mac. The rest of the laptop is familiar: the Liquid Retina XDR display with an optional nano-texture finish, a 12MP Center Stage camera, Wi-Fi 7 and Bluetooth 6, and macOS 27, in space black or silver.

The iMac makes the bigger generational jump. Apple skipped the M5 for its all-in-one entirely, so this machine moves straight from M4 to M6. The $1,499 base model has a 10-core CPU and 10-core GPU, while the $1,699 model gets the full 12-core chip. Apple claims up to 2x faster graphics and up to 5x faster on-device AI than the M4 iMac, plus SSDs up to twice as fast. The 24-inch 4.5K display and the seven color options carry over unchanged.

## Why the chip is the story

M6 is Apple's pitch for AI that runs on your machine instead of in a data center. The chip adds Neural Accelerators inside each GPU core and doubles up the Neural Engine, and Apple says that combination is what makes local AI work, from Apple Intelligence features across macOS to running large language models on the device itself, faster while keeping data private.

That framing matters because it is the same direction the rest of the industry is moving: a recent article on this site covered OpenAI cutting its frontier training off from the live internet, and the common thread is a shift toward keeping sensitive AI work local and controlled. Apple is simply making the consumer version of that argument, and selling it as a laptop.

## What it means for you

If you bought an M5 MacBook Pro recently, this is not a reason to upgrade: the gains are incremental, and the machine around the chip is the same one. If you are on an M1 Mac or an Intel machine, the jump is much larger: Apple claims up to 11x faster graphics and up to 13x faster AI performance than M1 for the MacBook Pro, and the iMac leap from M4 is the first update to that machine in two years.

For iMac buyers, the choice that matters is the chip tier, not the color. The $1,699 model buys the full 12-core M6 and four Thunderbolt 4 ports instead of two, so it is the one to pick if the machine will do heavy creative or AI work. The base model is the better value for everyday use.

:::info On-device AI
On-device AI means the model runs on the chip inside your computer, not on a remote server. The practical benefits are speed for small tasks, working without an internet connection, and data that never leaves the machine. The limit is scale: the biggest models still need data center hardware.
:::

## What is still unclear

Two caveats before you buy. First, every performance figure in this article is Apple's own claim, measured in Apple's own testing. No independent reviews or benchmarks of these two machines were available at announcement time, so treat the "up to" numbers as ceilings, not guarantees.

:::peringatan
Do not confuse this announcement with the event Apple is holding on October 28. Bloomberg has reported that event is expected to feature a touchscreen MacBook Pro, but Apple has not announced such a product. If you are waiting for a redesigned Mac, wait for that event, not this announcement.
:::

Second, Apple announced pre-orders in 35 countries and regions but the announcement covered here does not confirm local pricing or availability for every market, so check Apple's local store before assuming the US prices or the November 6 date apply where you live.
