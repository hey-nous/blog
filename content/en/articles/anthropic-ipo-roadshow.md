## What happened

Anthropic launched the roadshow for its proposed ==initial public offering== on November 10, 2026, setting the terms for what would be one of the largest stock market debuts on record. The Claude maker plans to sell 825 million Class A shares at an expected $70 to $78 each, and has applied to list on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker ANT.

Of the 825 million shares, about 801.4 million are being sold by Anthropic itself and about 23.6 million by existing stockholders. The underwriters, led by Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, also get a 30-day option to buy up to 123.75 million more shares at the IPO price. At $70 to $78, the public sale alone would raise $57.75 billion to $64.35 billion.

The price range implies a valuation of up to about $1.404 trillion on a fully diluted basis if the shares list at the top of the range, according to TechCrunch's reading of the amended filing. That is up from the $965 billion valuation Anthropic reached in a May fundraise, but below the $2 trillion figure the company was reportedly targeting a couple of months ago. On a narrower share count, Reuters put the top-of-range value at about $1.02 trillion, so the headline number depends on how you count the shares.

## The filing behind the numbers

The amended S-1 filed the same day gives the context for the price. Anthropic said its ==run-rate revenue== reached $81 billion by the end of October, about $10 billion more than at the end of September. The filing also lists about $900.9 billion in purchase commitments and leases, substantially all of it for compute and infrastructure, and a nine-month net loss of $104 billion.

Alongside the public offering, seven chip and cloud companies have agreed to buy $26 billion of a separate class of Anthropic stock in a concurrent private placement at the IPO price. The buyers named in the filing are AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia, and Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. TSMC separately approved an investment of up to $500 million in Anthropic at the IPO price, according to The Hindu BusinessLine.

As a public benefit corporation, Anthropic has committed 5 percent of its shares to a new Public Benefit Fund, with more added as the company's value grows. The fund is a separate entity from the company, and none of its equity will be used for lobbying or political giving.

## What it means for you

For most readers, nothing changes this week. This is a proposed offering, not a done deal: Anthropic's registration statement has not yet become effective, so no shares can be sold until the SEC signs off. CNBC, citing people familiar with the plans, reported pricing is expected on November 18 with trading starting November 19, but Anthropic has confirmed neither date.

If you use Claude, the IPO matters in a slower way. A public Anthropic answers to public shareholders, and the filing makes the economics plain: the company is spending at a scale that dwarfs its revenue, with nearly $901 billion committed to compute and infrastructure. The $26 billion placement from its suppliers is the other side of that bet: the companies selling Anthropic its chips are also buying its stock.

:::peringatan
Do not read the $1.4 trillion figure as a settled value. It is the top of a proposed range on a fully diluted share count, and the range itself can move. The Financial Times reported, citing unnamed people, that bankers expect to price above the $70 to $78 range after heavy first-day demand, which could lift the valuation to $1.5 trillion or more. Anthropic has not confirmed that, and no new filing has changed the range. Until pricing is set, every valuation number is an estimate, not a fact.
:::

What is still unclear: the final price, the first trading day, and how much of the offering goes to retail investors. The Motley Fool's reading of the filing puts the retail share at up to about 20 percent. The co-founders are also selling: each of the seven is selling 3,378,378 shares, worth about $250 million apiece at the midpoint, while keeping control of the company. Up to 316 million shares could also be sold from the first trading day under an early unlock in the filing.
