## What happened

Nvidia announced on November 12, 2026 that ==Jensen Huang== has resigned as president, chief executive, and a member of its board, effective immediately.

Huang co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and led it for 33 years, from a graphics-chip startup to the company whose processors power most of the world's AI training. The announcement, filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission as an exhibit to a Form 8-K, says the board accepted his resignation on November 11. It gives no reason for the departure and names no new role for Huang at the company.

==Colette Kress==, Nvidia's executive vice president and chief financial officer since September 2013, will serve as interim chief executive while keeping her finance role until a permanent successor is named. The board also appointed ==Harvey C. Jones==, a longtime director and former chief executive of chip-design firm Synopsys, as its first-ever chair. The board said it is starting a search for a permanent CEO and will consider internal and external candidates, with no timeline disclosed.

The company said operations, customer commitments, and the product roadmap will continue without interruption, and confirmed its earnings schedule is unchanged: third-quarter fiscal 2027 results on Tuesday, November 17, at about 1:20 p.m. Pacific Time, followed by an earnings call at 2 p.m.

## The week that led here

The announcement itself is silent on why Huang left. The context comes from reporting, not from the company, and it should be read that way.

On Sunday, November 8, Nvidia's board said it had opened an independent investigation into reports that Huang had been targeted by a foreign intelligence effort, while expressing confidence in his leadership and saying it did not believe company secrets or national security had been compromised. The same day, Huang admitted to an extramarital affair, without naming the other party.

In the days that followed, The Wall Street Journal reported that the board was preparing to remove Huang, and the New York Times had first reported in October 2025 on a business connection between Huang and Alice Huang, a Chinese businesswoman and former executive at Nvidia customer Megaspeed whom U.S. officials had been investigating as a potential national security risk. CNN reported that Huang has been called to testify in a closed-door congressional hearing on December 1, with lawmakers saying they will subpoena him if he does not agree voluntarily. China's Foreign Ministry has called the espionage claims unsubstantiated, and Nvidia has not confirmed any of the reported allegations.

The board convened on Wednesday night to weigh Huang's future, and he was out the next morning.

:::peringatan
None of the reported allegations about Huang's conduct have been confirmed by Nvidia, and the company's announcement gives no reason for the resignation. Treat the reporting as reporting: the facts in this story are the resignation, the interim appointment, and the new chair. Everything about why is still unconfirmed by the company.
:::

## What it means for you

For most people, the practical question is whether Nvidia's AI chip business changes, and the honest answer is that nothing in the announcement says it will. Kress has run Nvidia's finances for 13 years and knows the business; the product roadmap, the earnings date, and customer commitments are all explicitly unchanged.

The market, however, has already reacted. Nvidia's shares fell 12.75% from the start of the week to Friday's close, erasing roughly $573 billion in market value, according to CNN. Nvidia is about 6% of the S&P 500 by value, so the slide reaches well beyond Nvidia shareholders into retirement accounts and index funds.

The first real test comes fast. Kress faces investors on the November 17 earnings call, five days after the announcement, and analysts will want to hear whether the leadership change affects chip supply, the data-center roadmap, or the company's dealmaking. The second test is the permanent CEO search, which has no disclosed deadline.

What is still unclear: why Huang resigned, whether the board's independent investigation has concluded or what it found, who the permanent CEO will be, and whether the transition affects any of Nvidia's partnerships or pending deals. The announcement answers none of those questions, and the answers will come from the board, the investigation, or the earnings call, not from speculation.
