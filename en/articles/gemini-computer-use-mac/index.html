Gemini can now run your Mac apps for you

Google added computer use to the Gemini app for Mac on November 19. Gemini can now click, type, and fill in forms across your local files, desktop apps, and the web, in a background window while you keep working.

21 Nov 2026  •  4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Google announced on November 19, 2026 that computer use is now in the Gemini app for Mac, letting Gemini complete multi-step tasks across local files, desktop apps, and websites.
  • Gemini clicks, types, navigates menus, and fills in forms inside an isolated background window, with a live preview in chat, while your screen and cursor stay free.
  • You choose which apps Gemini can access, watch every step live, pause at any time, and approve key actions before they happen.
  • The rollout is in English in the US, for Google AI Plus, Pro, and Ultra subscribers, and requires an Apple silicon Mac.

What happened

Google announced on November 19, 2026 that computer use is now part of the Gemini app for Mac. Instead of answering in chat, Gemini can now do multi-step work across your local files, desktop apps, and websites: clicking buttons, typing into fields, navigating menus, and filling in forms, all from one plain-language request.

The examples Google gives are deliberately boring, which is the point. Gemini can batch color-grade photos in a design app, pull weights and addresses out of local PDFs to fill in multi-box shipping quotes, turn metrics and screenshots from live dashboards into a Google Slides deck, or work through a folder of clothing photos, research prices, and stage resale listings. One demo shows it taking a child's butterfly sketch and modelling and rigging it in Blender.

How it actually works

The key design choice is that Gemini does not take over your screen. It works inside an isolated background window, with a live preview inside your Gemini chat, so your main screen and cursor stay completely free. You can keep typing, designing, or taking calls while the task runs.

Control stays with you in three ways, according to Google: you choose which apps Gemini can access, you can watch every step live and pause at any time, and Gemini asks for approval before key actions happen. That last part matters, because an agent that can click through forms and listings can also do things you did not intend, and Google's own support documentation warns that agents can run into misleading instructions inside apps and websites.

Computer use

Computer use is the name for AI that operates a computer the way a person does, by looking at the screen and clicking, typing, and scrolling, instead of calling a purpose-built API. Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's operator-style tools work the same way. The trade-off is flexibility against risk: a screen-driving agent can use almost any app, but it can also be fooled by what is on the screen.

The launch is narrower than the headline suggests. Google says computer use is rolling out in English in the US, in the Gemini app for Mac, downloadable at gemini.google/mac. MacRumors reports it is available to Google AI Plus, Pro, and Ultra subscribers, with plans starting at $4.99 a month, and the app requires an Apple silicon Mac. There is no word yet on when it reaches other countries or the free tier.

Summary

Gemini on Mac can now do multi-step desktop work in a background window: clicking, typing, and filling in forms across files, apps, and the web. You pick which apps it can touch, watch it live, and approve key actions. For now it is US English only, for paid Google AI subscribers on Apple silicon Macs.

What it means for you

This is the moment AI agents stop living in a browser tab and start living on your desktop. Until now, the most capable agents mostly worked inside one app or one website. A computer-use agent can chain work across apps, which is how most real chores actually look: a folder of photos, a pricing search, a listing form, a spreadsheet.

It also arrives just as Apple is reportedly building its own version. Bloomberg reported on November 19 that Apple is hiring to build an AI agent that browses the web, works across apps, writes code, and calls services, possibly launching as soon as the end of next year. And Apple's next macOS release, 27.3, is expected to warn users more clearly about what an app can see when it controls other apps, with a visible indicator while an app is in control. Google getting there first on the Mac is a timing play as much as a feature launch.

For Mac users in the US with a paid Google AI plan, the practical test is simple: pick one repetitive multi-app chore and let Gemini do it while you watch. The live preview and the approval step are the safety net, and they are worth using, because an agent that fills in forms is only as careful as the permissions you give it.

What is still unclear: when computer use leaves the US and the paid tiers, whether Google brings the same capability to Windows, and how well the approval step holds up against prompt injection in real apps and websites. Google has not published accuracy or failure rates for the feature, so treat the first few runs as supervised experiments, not autopilot.
